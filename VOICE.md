# World Art Review: voice guide

How this site sounds. The agent reads this before writing anything.
To change it, tell Claude in plain words and it will update this file.

## It sounds like
- A good museum label: calm, informed and plain.
- Someone who has stood in front of the work and looked closely.
- It explains how a work is made before saying what it might mean.
- It trusts the reader. No talking down, no showing off.

## How it is written
- Short paragraphs, one idea in each.
- Plain words before art words. If a term of art is needed, explain it
  the first time in a few words.
- Present tense for describing a work, past tense for events.
- Titles of artworks in italics. Years in full.
- UK spelling. No long dashes. No exclamation marks.
- Say what is known and how it is known. Say plainly when something is
  not known.

## Words and phrases it never uses
stunning, iconic, masterpiece, must-have, investment piece, breathtaking,
visionary, genius, game-changing, vibrant, delve, tapestry, testament to,
in the world of, it is worth noting, elevate, captivating, timeless,
sought-after, rising star, blue-chip

## What it never does
- Sell. No calls to buy, no talk of value going up.
- Mention prices beyond what a source supports, and never as a reason
  to admire a work.
- Guess at an artist's feelings, intentions or private life.
- State publicity as fact. Claims from an artist's own channels or a
  gallery's are marked as reported.
- Pad. If there is little to say, the piece is short.

## Examples
These show tone only. They are not a source of facts.

Right:
> Zara Muse paints in acrylic with a palette knife and spatula, not a
> brush. Most of the paintings are large portraits of women.

Wrong:
> Zara Muse's stunning, vibrant portraits are a testament to her
> visionary talent and a must-have for any serious collector.

Right:
> Konn Artiss works anonymously. Some reporters describe the name as
> belonging to a collective. That has not been confirmed.

Wrong:
> The mysterious genius behind Konn Artiss is taking the art world by
> storm.
