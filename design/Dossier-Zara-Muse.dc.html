Zara Muse

A London painter of women's portraits, made with palette knives and spatulas alone. Her thick, sculptural surfaces carry a story about strength, loss and starting again.

Biography

Before she painted, Alexandra Johnson worked as a trader. She has described that world as hard, fast and short on feeling, and has said she is a gentler person now. [1]

The turn came with the death of her mother, to whom she was very close. In interview she links that loss directly to the determination that drove her into painting, working under the name Zara Muse. [1]

Her portraits have since been hung in luxury hotels, including sites run by the Fairmont group, and in Kanishka, a restaurant in Mayfair. Limited edition prints followed, to bring the work to a wider audience, and collectors in Dubai have begun to buy it. [2]

"I want to champion and support women."Zara Muse, speaking to Luxury News Online, 2025 [2]

Style and technique

Zara Muse paints only with palette knives and spatulas, never brushes. She has called the choice instinctive, made for the depth and richness it gives the surface. [1]

The result is paint that stands proud of the canvas. Colour is laid in thick, flat-edged slabs rather than blended, so a cheek or a jawline is built from separate planes of saturated colour. Seen close up the face breaks into ridges; from across a room it comes back together.
